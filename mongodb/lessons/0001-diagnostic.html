MongoDB Internals · Lesson 0001

Where are you on the stack?

A 20-question diagnostic, from basics to hard, so every later lesson lands in your zone.

MongoDB is a stack of layers. A request goes in through the wire protocol and the transport layer, gets dispatched as a command, passes through the query and replication layers, and reaches the storage engine, WiredTiger, which handles caching, durability and the bytes on disk. The full picture is in the architecture map. Keep it open in another tab.

This quiz samples every layer at three difficulty tiers. It is not graded. It's a probe. Two rules make it useful:

  1. Don't guess. If you don't know, press "Don't know: show me". A lucky guess hides a gap I should teach.
  2. Read every explanation, even when you're right. Each one cites the source it comes from.

Part 2 · Free recall (answer in the terminal)

Multiple choice tests recognition. These test whether you can produce the knowledge, which is what your mission needs (debugging, designing, teaching). Write a few sentences each, from memory, no searching. Paste them to me with your results.

  1. Trace a write. Your app calls insertOne(doc) with { w: "majority" }. List every layer and step you can think of, from the driver until the app gets its acknowledgement.
  2. Explain to a junior. In three sentences, what is the difference between a checkpoint and the journal, and why does MongoDB need both?
  3. Defend a decision. MongoDB secondaries pull the oplog instead of the primary pushing it. What do you think this buys, and what does it cost?

Blank answers are useful too. They tell me where to start.
